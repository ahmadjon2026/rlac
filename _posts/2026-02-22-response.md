**Interpretation:** Perplexity reads the bar chart as showing that the canonical Rowling texts rely much more on authority and institution words than on agency or self-direction words. In fanfiction, the balance is closer, with slightly more emphasis on agency. The heatmap reinforces this pattern by showing that keywords cluster differently across the two sets of texts: in Rowling’s novels, words like rules, ministry, and professor dominate, highlighting institutional and hierarchical language, while fanfiction emphasizes words like decide, chosen, lead, and headmaster, which relate to choice and action. Perplexity interprets these visuals as reflecting differences in how the texts frame authority and agency: the canonical texts focus on formal structures and control, while fanfiction gives characters more autonomy and personal initiative. Perplexity also notes that because stopwords and character names were removed, these patterns highlight content-word themes rather than general language or specific characters. Overall, Perplexity concludes that the canonical novels are institution-heavy, emphasizing hierarchy and formal power, while fanfiction is agency-heavy, foregrounding decision-making and self-directed actions.

**Interpretation:** Perplexity sees the Voyant trend as showing relative frequencies of plan, lead, decide, and control across texts. It notes that plan peaks sharply in one fanfiction text, lead rises toward later documents, decide shows a steady upward trend, and control remains low and stable. Perplexity interprets this as indicating that fanfiction emphasizes planning, decision-making, and leadership more variably and intensely than the canonical novels, which show steadier, moderate frequencies. It also emphasizes that, since stopwords and character names were removed, these trends reflect thematic language rather than mere stylistic choices or repeated character names. According to Perplexity, this pattern suggests that fanfiction gives characters more autonomy and agency, with amplified use of words tied to personal initiative, while canonical texts maintain controlled plotting and structured narrative arcs. The observed trends support the idea that fanfiction reshapes the narrative universe to highlight individual choice and leadership, contrasting with the institution-focused language of the canonical texts.

## Reflection
In this response, I used Perplexity AI to interpret the visuals from my Assignment 1 analysis. In general, Perplexity captured the broad patterns I observed in the distant reading: canonical Harry Potter texts emphasize institutional authority, while fanfiction slightly favors agency and self-directed action. The bar chart, heatmap, and Voyant trend visualizations all suggested that fanfiction gives characters more freedom and that canonical novels rely heavily on formal structures, which aligns with my original expectations.

However, Perplexity didn’t notice one crucial nuance that emerged in my Assignment 1: the strong authority gap is largely driven by a single word, “professor.” Words like “ministry” and “headmaster” remain relatively stable across both corpora. This shows that the apparent dominance of institutional language is not evenly distributed, a detail that distant reading alone, or Perplexity’s interpretation, can miss. Similarly, Perplexity recognized that agency terms such as “plan,” “decide,” and “lead” appear more in fanfiction, but it did not fully reflect that the increase is uneven. For example, “plan” peaks in one fanfiction text, while the other texts stay close to the canonical baseline.

Overall, Perplexity was helpful in capturing the general distribution patterns of authority and agency language, reinforcing most of the observations I made in Assignment 1. Yet, it missed the finer points, especially the fact that a single word can dominate trends and that shifts in agency are uneven. This aligns with what the High Theory podcast suggests: distant reading tools work at a macro level but still require human interpretation to fully understand subtleties and context.

In sum, this exercise shows that AI tools like Perplexity can support analysis by quickly summarizing broad trends, but they cannot replace careful, human interpretation, especially when one or two words shape the overall picture. Combining AI output with close reading provides a more balanced and accurate understanding of textual patterns.
