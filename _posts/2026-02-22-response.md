## Introduction
For Assignment 1, I analyzed three canonical Harry Potter novels by J.K. Rowling and three fanfiction stories set in the same universe. I focused on how language around authority and agency differs between the two groups. And, for the Response 1, to interpret the visuals, I used Perplexity AI in two separate chats. This was deliberate so the AI would not have prior context, allowing independent interpretations for each set of visuals.

The first chat focused on the bar chart and heatmap, which I sent together as two visuals. I asked four questions about them. The second chat focused on the Voyant Trends line chart, where I asked three additional questions about word frequency trends for plan, lead, decide, and control.

**Interpreation:** Perplexity reads the bar chart as showing that the canonical Rowling texts rely much more on authority and institution words than on agency or self-direction words. In fanfiction, the balance is closer, with slightly more emphasis on agency. The heatmap reinforces this pattern by showing that keywords cluster differently across the two sets of texts: in Rowling’s novels, words like rules, ministry, and professor dominate, highlighting institutional and hierarchical language, while fanfiction emphasizes words like decide, chosen, lead, and headmaster, which relate to choice and action. Perplexity interprets these visuals as reflecting differences in how the texts frame authority and agency: the canonical texts focus on formal structures and control, while fanfiction gives characters more autonomy and personal initiative. Perplexity also notes that because stopwords and character names were removed, these patterns highlight content-word themes rather than general language or specific characters. Overall, Perplexity concludes that the canonical novels are institution-heavy, emphasizing hierarchy and formal power, while fanfiction is agency-heavy, foregrounding decision-making and self-directed actions.
